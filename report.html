Tenet: typed tests for AI policies

Shane Haynes · MGT 409, Yale SOM, Fall 2026 · Repository: https://github.com/shanehaynes/tenet · Runs locally (no hosted link); an archived real run opens at /runs/example.

Tenet reads an AI agent's written policy, sorts each rule by the kind of rule it is, runs the test that kind demands, and reports where the agent departs from the policy and where the policy contradicts itself.

1. Motivation

Draft (Saturday): the platform lead who cannot verify that an agent follows its policy; what teams do today; the real policy owner's situation (M18).

2. What is new

Draft (Saturday): pass/fail is a category error; promptfoo, Giskard, garak, Patronus, Haize Labs, UK AISI Inspect, Braintrust, LangSmith Evals and OpenAI Evals are harnesses where you bring the tests. Tenet's new part is the rule-type taxonomy and the ambiguity map.

4. Tokenomics

The demo run made 158 model calls: 231,540 input tokens (190,154 cached), 29,160 output tokens including 11,432 reasoning tokens, for $0.0206 ($0.0025 analysis, $0.0181 tests) in 8 seconds of testing. Caching served 82.1 % of input tokens; without it the run would have cost $0.0377 (45.4 % more). At four policies tested three times a month per user, that is $0.25 per user per month, $24.74 at 100 users and $2474 at 10,000. Classifying all rules in one call instead of one call per rule: 90.7 % agreement, $0.0055 over 5 policies versus 86.7 % agreement, $0.0112 over 5 policies. Cumulative spend on the course key while building: $0.12 over 883 calls.

5. Safety

The injected copy of the demo policy is quarantined before any agent sees it; a run against a $0.01 test budget halts and says why; the judge ignores instructions planted in transcripts (2 of 2 canaries pass). In the demo run 0 verdicts had unverified evidence and 0 scenarios did not run.
